- Alert → incident correlation
- Incident response tasks (investigate/contain/eradicate)
- Threat intel enrichment

### Fields — critical vs useful vs optional

**Critical** (get these wrong and the module misbehaves; mandatory-ness varies by instance):

- `category (security incident)` — Drives the response playbook and required fields.
- `priority` — Derived from risk/severity; gates escalation.
- `assignment_group (SOC tier)` — Tier routing is the SOC operating model.
- `state` — Investigate → contain → eradicate → recover → review; review cannot be skipped for compliance.
- `observables (IOCs)` — The evidence chain; without them, enrichment and reporting are empty.

**Useful:** `cmdb_ci`, `business_impact`, attack techniques (MITRE mapping), related alerts

**Optional / nice to have:** external ticket ref, regulatory reporting flags

**Related lists worth configuring:** `Alerts`, `Tasks`, `Observables`, Affected CIs, Post-incident review

## Must know (the 5 facts that unlock the rest)

- Alerts are not incidents. Correlation rules turn N alerts into 1 incident — that rule set is the product.
- Playbooks (Process Automation Designer) are the response backbone; the incident form is just the record.
- Observables (IOCs) are child records used for enrichment and threat intel matching.

## Common mistakes

- Turning every alert into an incident (alert fatigue plus a useless MTTR).
- Skipping post-incident review state to 'close faster', losing compliance evidence.
- Loading intel feeds without tuning observables first.
